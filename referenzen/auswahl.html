Choice 1

Which websites should we learn from?

Pick one or two. For each: what we would take and what we would leave. We copy ideas, never designs or words.

Plum Guide plumguide.com · curated holiday homes

A hand-picked collection of homes with a strong editorial voice.

Take
Search bar in the first screen (for later, with the PMS), big calm serif headline, "only the best homes" curation idea.
Leave
Award badges, bright yellow button.

Le Collectionist lecollectionist.com · luxury villa collection

Exactly your concept at a bigger scale: a collection of homes under one brand.

Take
"Our homes" grid with simple categories, quiet luxury, lots of white space, small caps labels.
Leave
Chalet look, crowded top bar.

Our Habitas ourhabitas.com · soulful hotels

The landscape does the talking. Full-width photo, almost no text in the first screen.

Take
Landscape-led first screen (your karst view), warm earthy mood, minimal navigation.
Leave
Hidden menu, vague copy, heavy cookie banner.

Namal namal.ph · El Nido, 3 properties

The local example of an umbrella brand: "Three hotels, one island experience."

Take
One clear line about the collection, a button per property.
Leave
The thin page (one sentence), dark photo, nothing for Google to read.

House Formo houseformo.com · El Nido, design-led

The closest in spirit: "warm minimal luxury", geometry and tactile materials.

Take
Close-up texture photos, elegant serif, calm pace, "A walk through the spaces".
Leave
Grey, low-contrast text, very little content.

Airbnb the pattern guests already know

You mentioned it: guests understand cards with a photo, name, rating and a short line.

Take
Card layout for the stays, rating shown on each card, dates picker later.
Leave
The marketplace look. This should feel like your home, not a listing site.
Choice 2

Colours and typefaces

All three start from your interiors. They differ in which second colour leads: your wood, the karst green, or the lagoon teal of your villa sofa. The typeface pairs can be swapped between palettes. All text colours pass accessibility contrast (WCAG AA).
